# AI-related breach costs — source and interpretation

Source: **IBM, Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025**, research conducted by Ponemon Institute. Figure 31, printed page 37 (PDF page 19 in the spread-layout copy).

- [Full report, hosted by Ingram Micro](https://na.ingrammicro.com/Ingram/media/North-America-US/EN-US/I/ibm/docs/IBM-cost-of-a-data-breach-2025-full-report.pdf#page=19)
- [IBM's official announcement, 30 July 2025](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-07-30-ibm-report-13-of-organizations-reported-breaches-of-ai-models-or-applications,-97-of-which-reported-lacking-proper-ai-access-controls)

| Category | Average cost, USD millions |
|---|---:|
| Incident targeting an AI model | 4.46 |
| AI used to execute the security incident | 4.49 |
| Unsanctioned / shadow AI involved | 4.63 |

The overall study covered 600 breached organizations and incidents from March 2024 to February 2025. Figure 31 does not display per-category sample counts or confidence intervals. Treat categories as potentially overlapping; do not add their costs together.

These are total breach costs, not just stolen funds. The study concerns AI-related security incidents, not browser automation specifically. It does not establish that automation caused these costs or that Dev Privacy Guard would prevent or save these amounts.

Suggested presentation sentence: “IBM reports an average breach cost of US$4.63 million when unauthorized AI is involved, highlighting the need to govern how workplace AI handles sensitive data.”
